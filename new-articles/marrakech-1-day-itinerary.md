# Marrakech in One Day: Itinerary Including Bahia Palace

You have one day. You are probably arriving from Essaouira, Agadir or a cruise stop, you have maybe eight usable hours, and every itinerary you have read lists eleven things.

You cannot do eleven things. You can do four properly, and you will enjoy that far more than sprinting between all of them.

This is an hour-by-hour plan for Marrakech in one day, built around the southern medina, where the monuments sit close enough together to walk. It assumes a 9:00 start and a sunset finish. Real walking times, real prices, and a note at the end about which stop to cut when you inevitably run late.

![Jemaa el-Fna square in Marrakech with market stalls and the Koutoubia minaret behind](placeholder-image.jpg)

## Can you actually see Marrakech in one day?

You can see one district of Marrakech properly in one day. That district should be the southern medina, and it should include Bahia Palace, the Saadian Tombs, a Kasbah lunch and Jemaa el-Fna at sunset.

What you are giving up: the Majorelle Garden and the Ville Nouvelle, both of which are a taxi ride away in the other direction. Trying to add Majorelle to this plan means an hour of transit and a timed-entry queue, and it is the single most common way one-day visitors ruin their day. If gardens matter more to you than palaces, our [Bahia versus Majorelle comparison](/en/blog/bahia-palace-vs-majorelle-garden) will help you swap rather than add.

## 9:00 · Bahia Palace

Start here, at opening, and do not negotiate with yourself about it.

Bahia Palace opens daily at 9:00 and closes at 17:00. The first hour is the quietest of the day by a wide margin: tour groups arrive from about 10:30, and by midday the ticket queue can run 20 to 30 minutes in high season. Arriving at 9:00 typically means walking straight in.

Entry is 100 MAD for foreign adults, roughly ten dollars. Children under seven are free. Tickets are sold on site under the Ministry of Culture, and you can check the official portal at e-services.minculture.gov.ma. Our [entrance fee guide](/en/blog/bahia-palace-entrance-fee-2026) covers where people overpay.

Give it 75 to 90 minutes. The palace covers roughly 8,000 m² on a single level, and you will walk about a kilometre inside without noticing. The rooms are empty, which surprises people, and the reason is worth knowing before you arrive: the palace was stripped shortly after Ba Ahmed died in 1900. Our [history of the building](/en/blog/bahia-palace-history) is a five-minute read that changes what you are looking at.

If you want to be efficient with your 90 minutes, the [room-by-room guide](/en/blog/bahia-palace-room-by-room-guide) tells you which courtyards are worth the time and which you can pass through.

**Getting there:** Bahia is on Rue Riad Zitoun el Jedid, about ten minutes' walk from Jemaa el-Fna. If your day starts with a taxi drop at the square, walk it. Our [directions guide](/en/blog/how-to-get-to-bahia-palace-marrakech) has the route.

## 10:45 · Walk to the Saadian Tombs

Twelve minutes on foot, southwest through the Kasbah. You will pass the walls of El Badi on the way.

A decision point here. If you are more interested in scale and ruins than in tombs, substitute [El Badi Palace](/en/blog/bahia-palace-vs-badi-palace-marrakech), which is closer and less crowded. If you want the finest small space in the southern medina, stay with the Saadian Tombs.

## 11:00 · Saadian Tombs

Forty-five minutes, and the timing matters more here than anywhere else on this list.

The tombs are a compact site and the main chamber, the one everyone photographs, is small. Queues to enter that chamber build steadily from about 11:30 and by early afternoon you can wait 20 minutes for a two-minute look. Arriving at 11:00 usually gets you in with a short wait.

Our guide to [combining Bahia Palace and the Saadian Tombs in one morning](/en/blog/bahia-palace-and-saadian-tombs-one-day) has more detail on making this pairing work.

## 12:00 · The Kasbah and the Mellah

You now have 45 minutes before lunch and two good options.

**Option A: the Mellah.** Marrakech's historic Jewish quarter is a two-minute walk from Bahia and has the best-value market in the medina, plus taller buildings and street balconies you will not see elsewhere. Our [Mellah guide](/en/blog/mellah-marrakech-jewish-quarter) covers what to look for.

**Option B: just walk the Kasbah.** Less structured, no entry fees, and honestly this is where a lot of the character of the southern medina lives.

If you are behind schedule, this is the stop to cut. It is the only one on the list that has no fixed opening hours and no queue.

## 13:00 · Lunch

Eat in this area rather than walking north for it. The Kasbah has the concentration of restaurants near the monuments, from rooftop terraces to small local places, and our [restaurants near Bahia Palace](/en/blog/restaurants-near-bahia-palace) guide covers the range.

Two honest warnings. Rooftop terraces in this area charge a real premium for the view and the food is frequently ordinary; at 1pm in summer you are paying extra to sit in the sun. And service in the medina is unhurried by design, so a sit-down lunch takes 60 to 75 minutes whether you planned for it or not. Build that in.

The cheap and genuinely good alternative is to buy bread, olives, dates and fruit in the Mellah market and eat as you walk. Twenty minutes, a fraction of the price.

## 14:30 · The souks

Walk north from the Kasbah toward Jemaa el-Fna and into the souks behind it. Allow 90 minutes, which sounds generous and is not, because you will get lost.

You will get lost. Everyone does, the lanes genuinely are a maze, and mapping apps lose the plot under the covered sections. Treat it as the activity rather than a problem, and know that walking consistently downhill and north-west brings you back toward the square eventually.

A few practical things. Prices are negotiable and the opening number is usually well above what the seller expects. Be ready for the "this street is closed" routine, which is how unofficial guides start a conversation; our [Marrakech safety guide](/en/blog/marrakech-safety-guide) covers the common approaches and how to end them politely without being rude.

If haggling makes you uncomfortable, and it makes plenty of people uncomfortable, it is a normal social exchange here rather than a confrontation.

## 16:30 · Stop moving

Sit down. Order a mint tea. Do not skip this.

Between June and September you will have been walking on stone in direct sun for seven hours by this point, and the difference between people who enjoy Jemaa el-Fna at sunset and people who endure it is entirely down to whether they rested at 16:30. Any café on the square's edge with a terrace works. You are buying the seat and the view.

## 17:30 · Jemaa el-Fna at sunset

This is the payoff, and it is genuinely worth building the day around.

The square changes character completely in the hour around sunset. The daytime layout of juice carts and snake charmers gives way to rows of food stalls that are assembled in about thirty minutes flat. Smoke, lights, crowds, and the Koutoubia minaret going gold behind it all.

Find a rooftop terrace on the square's northern edge for the view from above, then come down into it. Both perspectives are worth having and the transition takes ten minutes.

On eating at the stalls: it is fine, and the food is generally good and cheap. Pick a stall that is busy with locals, ignore anyone who physically steers you toward a table, and confirm prices before ordering rather than after. Some stalls bring "complimentary" bread and olives that are not complimentary.

## What to skip

Being direct about this, because one-day visitors are told to see everything:

**The Majorelle Garden.** Beautiful, and in the wrong direction. Getting there and back costs 60 to 90 minutes plus a timed queue. It does not fit this day.

**Ben Youssef Madrasa.** A genuinely superb building, but it is in the northern medina and pulls you away from the southern cluster. Our [comparison with Bahia Palace](/en/blog/bahia-palace-vs-ben-youssef-madrasa) explains the trade-off if you would rather swap.

**A camel ride in the Palmeraie.** Half a day, and it is not Marrakech.

**A hammam.** Wonderful, and two hours you do not have.

**More than one museum.** Pick one monument in the morning, one in the late morning, and stop.

## If you have two days instead

Then a lot of this pressure disappears and the plan changes shape rather than just getting longer. Our [Marrakech two-day itinerary](/en/blog/marrakech-2-day-itinerary) puts the southern medina on day one and adds Majorelle, Ben Youssef and the northern souks on day two, at a pace that allows lunch to take as long as it takes.

If you can extend to two days, do. One day in Marrakech works, but it works by being disciplined about what it excludes.

## Practical notes for a one-day visit

**Cash.** Carry dirhams in small denominations. Monuments, taxis and market stalls are cash-first, and nobody has change for a 200 note at 9am.

**Shoes.** Medina paving is uneven and you will cover 8 to 12 km. This is not the day for new sandals.

**Dress.** Shoulders and knees covered gets you into every monument without discussion. Our [dress code guide](/en/blog/bahia-palace-dress-code) has the specifics.

**Water.** Carry more than you think, especially April to October.

**Ramadan.** If your day falls during Ramadan, monument hours shorten and the rhythm of the city changes substantially. Our [Ramadan visiting guide](/en/blog/bahia-palace-during-ramadan) explains what to shift.

**Heat.** From June, the 13:00 to 16:00 window is genuinely punishing in open courtyards. This itinerary deliberately puts you under a roof or in the shade during those hours.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is one day enough for Marrakech?**
One day is enough to see the southern medina properly: Bahia Palace, the Saadian Tombs, the Kasbah, the souks and Jemaa el-Fna at sunset. It is not enough to add the Majorelle Garden or the northern medina without rushing everything.

**What time should I start a one-day Marrakech itinerary?**
Nine in the morning, at Bahia Palace as it opens. The first hour is by far the quietest at the monuments, and starting later pushes you into both the midday queues and the afternoon heat.

**How much does a day in Marrakech cost?**
Entry to Bahia Palace is 100 MAD for foreign adults. The Saadian Tombs are ticketed separately. Add lunch, mint tea and any purchases in the souks. Monuments themselves are among the cheaper parts of the day.

**Can I visit Bahia Palace and the Saadian Tombs on the same day?**
Comfortably. They are about twelve minutes' walk apart in the southern medina. Do Bahia at 9:00 and the Saadian Tombs at 11:00, before the queue at the main chamber builds.

**Is Jemaa el-Fna worth visiting at sunset?**
It is the best hour of the day there. The square transforms as the food stalls are set up, and the light on the Koutoubia is the image most people carry away from Marrakech. Rest beforehand so you can enjoy it.

**Should I book a guide for one day in Marrakech?**
Not necessary for this route, which is walkable and self-explanatory. A guide adds most value inside Bahia Palace, where the rooms are empty and the story is not written on the walls. An [audio guide](/en/blog/bahia-palace-audio-guide) is the cheaper way to get the same benefit.
